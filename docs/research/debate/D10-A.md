# D10-A: FOR the approval gate (private/unlisted -> one-click human approve)

Note: my live re-fetch of support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392 was rate-limited; all facts below come from the research reports (10-policy, 07-upload, 09-orchestration), where [V] = verified there.

## Thesis
Gate every video behind a ~2-minute approve tap, and flip to autopilot only after a clean record. The gate costs $0 and under 5 minutes a day. The risk it removes is channel-level and slow to reverse.

## Arguments
1. **Monetization is judged per channel, not per video.** Too much generic, templated or mass-produced content costs the whole channel its monetization (netinfluencer, Jul 2026, [S] in 10-policy). Suspension means a 21-day appeal, then reapplying after 90 days. One bad batch of unreviewed uploads can cost months. A human can reject a samey video before it counts against the channel.
2. **The automated gates cannot judge the things most likely to hurt.** The codeable gates in 10-policy §3 check similarity, licenses and sources. Risk #4 (factual errors in technical content) is rated High likelihood and High impact. Its only mitigation is "human sign-off", and its source is marked UNVERIFIED. The sensitive-topic classifier can also miss a video.
3. **The gate is nearly free and already designed.** 10-policy §4 estimates 2-5 minutes per approval with the pipeline otherwise 95% automatic. 09-orchestration already has an `approval` state, `awaiting_approval` status, a Telegram alert, and an upload stage that "refuses without approval=approved". It also has a test that a run with no approval makes zero upload calls. The 07-upload spec already uploads as `private` by default. Little extra work is needed.
4. **It covers an API risk nobody has resolved.** The private-lock rule is CONFLICTING (07-upload §1). The Help Center says unverified API uploads can be locked with no appeal. The API reference says they are not. A private-first flow with a human flip means a locked or broken video is caught before any audience sees it.
5. **It leaves an audit trail.** Recorded approvals support appeals and the AI-disclosure decision (10-policy §4, Option Y). Repeated non-disclosure can lead to YPP suspension ([V] answer/14328491).

## Strongest opposing point and answer
"A human in the loop is not full automation, and rubber-stamp fatigue makes it useless." Answer: the human step is one tap, and the report is generated automatically. The automatic checklist can show the sources, similarity score, flags and a render preview. It should also keep a reject rate and a time-to-approve log, so rubber-stamping shows up in the data. Opt-in autopilot is built in: after N clean approvals (for example 30 videos with no flags, a zero-reject window and no policy warnings), the same code path can auto-approve. The gate is a config flag, not a rewrite.

## What would change my mind
- Evidence that private-then-public or unlisted-then-public triggers a reach or ranking penalty (none found; UNVERIFIED either way).
- A 30-video trial in which the approver never rejects and no human-caught defect would have been missed by the automated gates.
- YouTube stating that per-video human review is not a factor for inauthentic-content assessment. Note that the Halprin statement (Jul 2026, [S]) says originality matters, not review itself.

## Scores (1-5; effort 5 = easiest)
- Cost: 5 (about $0; Telegram bot is free)
- Output quality: 5
- Policy risk: 5 (lowest of the options; the reviewer is the main defense)
- Implementation effort: 4 (the state machine and approval stage are already specced; Telegram or email glue is small)
- Maintainability: 4 (one flag, one extra command)
