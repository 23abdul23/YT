# D10-B (AGAINST mandatory pre-publish human approval)

Note: my fetches of YouTube pages were rate-limited (Firecrawl); all facts below come from the context reports (10-policy.md, 07-upload.md) and are labelled as such.

## Thesis
Day-one design: auto-publish when all automated gates pass, with a veto-window (upload private, auto-flip public after N hours unless the owner vetoes), a global kill switch, and post-publish auto-unlist on anomalies. A mandatory per-video approval contradicts the stated goal (FULLY automated) and is not what the policy evidence requires.

## Arguments
1. **Policy does not require a human step.** The 10-policy.md summary of Halprin's 2026-07 statement says rules apply whether made with "AI, CGI, or no additional tools" and evaluation is channel-level, based on content (templated/mass-produced, manipulative, AI personas on finance/legal/medical). The three buckets are measurable properties of output, which gates can check (variety, topic block). The report's claim that "human value-add is the defense" is its own inference. Option Y cites answer/1311392 for it, but that page's text, as quoted, speaks of original insight, not human review.
2. **Risk #1 (templated content) is not reduced by a rubber-stamp.** A 2-5 min approval cannot add originality. Gates 4-5 (similarity <0.8, structure variation, opinion/demo required) and the cadence cap (gate 8) attack it directly. Human review of 1-3 videos/day is tiring and degrades into click-through, giving false assurance.
3. **Latency kills the niche.** Tech explainers depend on trend timeliness (the premise of the plan). A video waiting on a human who is asleep or away misses the window. Requiring approval also makes the pipeline stall whenever the owner is on holiday or sick.
4. **Remaining human-only risks are covered by cheaper controls.** Wrong facts (risk #4): claim-check against cited source IDs (gate 5). Disclosure (risk #3): conservative auto flag; 14328491 (per report) says animated and AI-assisted scripts need no label. Sensitive topics: hard block (gate 3). Mistakes found afterwards: videos.update (50 units, 07-upload.md) flips privacy in seconds, and quota (10,000/day vs ~500/video) is no constraint. Post-publish auto-unlist plus kill switch bound the downside to hours.
5. **Audit trail does not need a human.** Manifest, asset ledger, gate logs and source IDs per video serve appeals as well as a click.

## Strongest point FOR D10, and my answer
Enforcement is channel-level, with no phase-in, 21-day appeal, 90-day reapply (10-policy.md), so one bad batch can end monetization, and a false negative from an LLM claim-check costs more than 3 minutes. Answer: true, so make the cost asymmetry explicit. Start with a 24-48h hold and an owner veto (async, not blocking), measure gate false-negative rate for ~30 videos, and keep the owner's veto notification. This keeps autonomy and gives the same safety net as D10 without making the human a required dependency.

## What would change my mind
- Official YouTube text that human review is required or materially protective for YPP (not found in the report; UNVERIFIED either way).
- Gate audit: >5% of 30 test videos have factual errors that claim-check missed.
- Any YouTube warning/strike or YPP rejection on the channel.
- Evidence that auto-unlisting after publication does not undo harm (e.g. fast strikes for misinformation).

## Scores (veto-window autopublish + gates + kill switch + auto-unlist)
cost 5, output quality 3, policy risk 3 (D10 would be about 4), implementation effort 3, maintainability 4.
