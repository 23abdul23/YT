# D11-A (FOR): LLM-authored scene code as the visuals backbone

## Thesis
Code-drawn scenes are the only option that is free to render, fits a 4 GB GPU, and is deterministic, auditable and syncable to words.json. A static-title fallback or a curated figure library cannot be both varied and automated.

## Arguments
1. **The alternative is the gap.** Plan Task 11 renders `diagram` as plain title text, and figures come from 3-6 hand-made Anatomy pieces (11-addendum G1; Plan.md Task 11 routing rule: 70-80% of scenes are fallback). Without a generator, most of every video is a text slide. That is the exact "slideshows ... minimal narrative" pattern in YouTube's inauthentic-content policy (https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392: "Image slideshows, templated storylines, or scrolling text").
2. **Per-video variation is what the policy rewards.** The same page requires each video's substance be "materially varied"; AI use is allowed when it supports your own creative work. Scene code authored from each script's own content differs every video; a reused library is the templated risk Plan D4-B raised. Policy has no animation carve-out, so variation is the defence.
3. **Infrastructure already exists.** The virtual-clock capture tool (`render-figure.mjs`, ~110 ms/1080p frame, Plan section 1) works on any HTML (addendum section 4). Moving subtitles before visuals puts `words.json` cues into the author prompt (addendum section 3, G2 fix). Reveals land on spoken words.
4. **QA is nearly free.** Haiku 5.5 is $0.10/MTok input, $0.50 output (https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing, read today). My arithmetic, not measured: 25 scenes x 3 frames x ~1.8k image tokens = ~135k tokens, about $0.014 per video. Repairs are capped at 2; then stock/text fallback, so worst case equals today's plan.
5. **Failure modes are known and loopable.** Research on LLM Manim finds the dominant errors are hallucinated APIs and LaTeX mistakes, mitigated by render-and-repair loops (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.19400, https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.16295). A grant proposal claims a visual feedback loop cuts errors from 65% to under 8% (https://manifund.org/projects/manimagent-an-open-25000-k12-video-library-; UNVERIFIED, self-reported). HTML/SVG is far better represented in training data than Manim, so make it the default and keep Manim for algorithm/graph scenes.

## Strongest opposing point and answer
Cost and reliability: addendum section 3 estimates $1.2-1.8 per long video, above the $1.50 cap, and Manim may not even install on this PC (Spike E). Answer: that estimate is unmeasured and ignores caching and Haiku for simple scenes; cap is a config value; the Task 16 pilot measures it. $1.8 x 8 videos = $14, within $25. Manim is optional: if Spike E fails, HTML-only still covers the backbone.

## What would change my mind
- Pilot shows over 40% of scenes hitting fallback after 2 repairs, or real cost above about $3 per video.
- Spike E fails and HTML scenes look generic across 5 sample topics.
- Haiku vision misses clipped text in a 20-frame test set.

## Scores (proposition)
- Cost: 3 (about $1.5-2.5/video vs ~$0.7)
- Output quality: 4
- Policy risk: 4 (lower risk than static slides, UNVERIFIED how YouTube judges it)
- Implementation effort: 2 (tasks 11a-d, sandboxing)
- Maintainability: 3 (Manim API drift; prompts need tests)
