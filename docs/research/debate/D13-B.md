# D13-B: Against Pexels in the visual fallback chain

## Thesis
Drop Pexels from the chain. The channel's differentiator (Plan.md R1, R12) is code-drawn visuals. Generic stock B-roll under TTS is the template look YouTube targets, and it adds a dependency for little gain. The fallback should be a well-designed code-drawn card (title, key terms, a simple diagram), not stock.

## Arguments
1. **Policy risk.** YouTube's page lists "Image slideshows, templated storylines, or scrolling text with minimal or no narrative" and "AI-generated content made with generic or unoriginal templates" as violations, and judges at channel level (https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392). Stock B-roll plus captions is the usual look of such channels. The page does not name stock footage, so this is my inference. A third-party summary says "endless compilations of stock content" won't qualify (https://sybrid.com/resources/blog/youtube-monetization-updates-2025/), a weak source.
2. **It contradicts the plan's own risk mitigation.** R1 relies on a "distinct Anatomy visual identity"; R12 relies on "no third-party clips" (Plan.md lines 1848, 1859). Any Pexels clip is a third-party clip, and the fallback fires on exactly the scenes that already failed QA (11-addendum line 93).
3. **Attribution is murky and probably unneeded.** The license says attribution "is not required" (https://www.pexels.com/license/). The API says Pexels and contributors "must be credited within your platform," and the rate-limit lift is "currently paused" (https://help.pexels.com/hc/en-us/articles/900005852323). The addendum (line 50) admits a description credit is UNVERIFIED. Per-video credit lines add description-template boilerplate, which is another repetitive signal.
4. **Runtime dependency.** The pipeline runs unattended. Pexels adds a key, a 200/hour and 20,000/month cap, outages, and a terms change risk. A local renderer fails only on our own bugs. The cost is $0 either way, but maintenance is not.
5. **Relevance.** Search queries for abstract tech topics return mismatched clips such as laptops and server racks. Vision QA (04c) then needs to judge relevance, which raises cost (addendum line 104 already shows the budget is tight). A text/diagram card is always on-topic.

## Strongest point FOR, and my answer
Real footage gives visual variety and may hold retention better than repeated text cards. Answer: variety can come from code, such as 4-6 card templates, animated SVG diagrams, and kinetic text driven by word timings. That is original and deterministic. Retention benefit is UNVERIFIED either way; test it in the Task 16 pilot.

## What would change my mind
- Written reply from api@pexels.com that description credits satisfy their terms.
- A pilot A/B showing stock-fallback scenes retain clearly better (over 10% relative) than designed cards.
- YouTube text explicitly saying that narrated stock B-roll with original script commentary is fine.
- Evidence that fewer than 5% of scenes ever reach the fallback; then the question hardly matters.
